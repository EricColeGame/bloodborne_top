## How This Bloodborne Weapon Tier List Is Built

Tiers are only useful if you know what they measure. This ranking assumes a +10 weapon, a character with roughly 40–50 in the relevant damage stat, and blood gems you can realistically farm without living in Chalice Dungeons. Lost and Uncanny variants are treated as the same weapon here — they share movesets and differ mainly in gem slots.

The criteria behind every placement:

- **Damage per stamina point** — how much punishment one bar buys you.
- **Moveset coverage** — access to horizontal sweeps, vertical swings, and thrust attacks for different enemy shapes.
- **Transform attacks** — the L1 chains that let a weapon flow between forms mid-combo.
- **Scaling ceiling** — how well the weapon rewards heavy stat investment.
- **Rally potential** — how much health you claw back after trading hits.
- **Practicality** — how early you get it and how forgiving it is to learn.

| Tier | What it means | Typical use case |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Best-in-class damage, moveset, or utility with no real weakness | Main weapon for a full playthrough |
| **A** | Excellent, but with one clear trade-off (speed, stamina, or stat spread) | Primary or high-value secondary |
| **B** | Solid and fun, usually outclassed in raw efficiency | Themed builds, PvP mix-ups, challenge runs |
| **C** | Niche or deliberately difficult | Off-meta and cosplay runs |

## S-Tier: The Weapons That Carry Runs

The top of any **Bloodborne weapon tier list** is crowded because FromSoftware balanced the roster unusually well. What separates S from A is rarely raw damage — it's the ability to answer every situation without swapping weapons.

The Saw twins earn their spot through speed and the serrated bonus against beasts, which applies to a huge share of the game's enemies. Community rankings consistently single out transform attacks as the deciding factor for the top tier, and both saw weapons have fast, high-damage L1 chains that slot neatly into combos. Ludwig's Holy Blade is the opposite approach: slower, but with thrust attacks and two-handed damage that trivializes many bosses. The Whirligig Saw's held grind attack melts large beast-type bosses, while the Holy Moonlight Sword adds ranged arcane projectiles to a quality build.

| Weapon | Scaling focus | Signature strength | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saw Cleaver | Strength-leaning quality | Fast transform attacks, serrated bonus | Short reach |
| Saw Spear | Skill-leaning quality | Serrated plus thrust, superb stamina economy | Modest base damage |
| Ludwig's Holy Blade | Quality (Str/Skill) | Huge two-handed damage and thrust R2 | Heavy stamina cost, slow swings |
| Whirligig Saw | Strength | Held grind attack shreds big beasts | You stand still while grinding |
| Holy Moonlight Sword | Quality plus Arcane | Ranged energy waves, strong arcane scaling | Demands arcane investment |
| Rakuyo | Skill | Blistering dual-blade combos | Low stagger, weak against poise |
| Beasthunter Saif | Skill | Transform attacks that close or open distance | Unremarkable raw numbers |
| Chikage | Skill plus Bloodtinge | Bloodtinge damage in transformed mode | Constant HP drain while transformed |

Two of these deserve a warning. The Chikage punishes sloppy play because its blood form bleeds your health, and the Rakuyo rewards aggression in a way that gets new hunters killed. They're S-tier in capable hands, B-tier in careless ones.

## A-Tier and B-Tier: Strong Picks With Trade-Offs

A-tier weapons are frequently someone's favorite, and for good reason — they simply ask for one concession. The Hunter Axe trades finesse for a charged spin that clears crowds and staggers almost anything. The Kirkhammer delivers the best pure Strength stagger in the game. Blades of Mercy produce some of the fastest sustained damage in the game but chew through durability. The Church Pick is quietly one of the best all-rounders thanks to thrust and serrated properties in both forms, and Simon's Bowblade gives Bloodtinge builds a genuine ranged option.

### Upgrade Costs and Blood Stone Priority

Weapon upgrades in Bloodborne follow a fixed material ladder. Because Blood Stone Rocks are scarce, your first +10 should go to the weapon you actually finish runs with.

| Upgrade range | Material | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| +1 to +3 | Blood Stone Shards | Plentiful from early Yharnam enemies |
| +4 to +6 | Twin Blood Stone Shards | Drops from mid-game enemies, buyable later |
| +7 to +9 | Blood Stone Chunks | Late-game areas and Chalice Dungeons |
| +10 | Blood Stone Rock | One guaranteed in the main game; more via Chalices and the Insight shop |

A practical tip: spread early shards across two weapons so you always have a fast option and a heavy option, then commit chunks and the Rock to one.

## FAQ

**What is the best weapon in Bloodborne for a first playthrough?**
The Saw Cleaver is the safest answer. It's a starting weapon, upgrades cheaply, hits fast, and gets a serrated bonus against beasts. If you prefer reach and staggering power, the Hunter Axe is an equally beginner-friendly choice from the same opening selection.

**Is the Saw Cleaver better than Ludwig's Holy Blade?**
They solve different problems. The Saw Cleaver wins on speed, stamina economy, and transform attacks; Ludwig's Holy Blade wins on raw two-handed damage and thrust attacks. Most players keep both upgraded and swap based on the boss.

**Do DLC weapons dominate this Bloodborne weapon tier list?**
Not entirely, but The Old Hunters adds several top-tier options — Whirligig Saw, Rakuyo, Beasthunter Saif, Holy Moonlight Sword, and Simon's Bowblade all rank highly. The base game still supplies the Saw Cleaver, Saw Spear, and Ludwig's Holy Blade, so you're never dependent on the DLC to build a strong character.

**How many weapons should I upgrade at once?**
Two is the sweet spot for most of the game: one fast weapon and one heavy hitter. Save Blood Stone Chunks and the Blood Stone Rock for the single weapon you trust most, and use the Insight shop to fill gaps once you're in the late game.
