## Why Skill Is the Thinking Hunter's Stat

Bloodborne rewards aggression, but it punishes sloppy aggression — and no playstyle makes that clearer than a **Bloodborne skill build**. Trading hits with a heavy Strength weapon works until it doesn't. A Skill hunter slips through the swing, parries the follow-up, and turns a single visceral attack into a deleted health bar. If you want a **Bloodborne skill build** that scales into the endgame and holds up in PvP, the payoff comes from timing rather than raw numbers.

Skill does two jobs at once. It raises the damage of Skill-scaling weapons, and it directly increases visceral attack damage — the riposte you land after a gun parry or a backstab. That second effect is why Skill builds feel so explosive against bosses and enemy hunters alike. You are not just swinging faster; every successful parry hits meaningfully harder than it would on a Strength character.

The tradeoff is real. Skill weapons tend to have lower per-hit damage and shorter reach on their quickest attacks, so mistakes are punished. This guide covers the stat targets, the weapons worth upgrading, and the level 120 setup that keeps the build competitive. For background on the game itself, see [the official Bloodborne page on PlayStation](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/bloodborne/).

## The Best Weapons for a Bloodborne Skill Build

Community reports and player experience consistently point to the same shortlist. What matters more than raw attack rating is how a weapon's moveset fits a dodge-heavy, parry-focused playstyle — transformation attacks, thrust options, and low stamina cost per swing.

| Weapon | Skill Scaling | Where to Get It | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threaded Cane | Moderate–High | Starting weapon | Whip form has long reach and serrated damage against beasts; the cane form is fast and cheap on stamina |
| Saw Spear | High | Central Yharnam sewers | Serrated bonus, strong thrust attacks, excellent transformation combos; the best early all-rounder |
| Beasthunter Saif | High | The Old Hunters DLC | Transformation attacks that lunge forward or back, letting you close or reset distance instantly |
| Blades of Mercy | Very High | Eileen the Crow's questline | Fastest attacking weapon in the game, tiny stamina cost, devastating dodge-attack chains |
| Church Pick | High | The Old Hunters DLC | Long reach and thrust-heavy attacks that punish large openings |
| Rakuyo | Very High | Fishing Hamlet (DLC) | Dual-blade combos with the highest sustained DPS of any Skill weapon |
| Burial Blade | High | Late game / Chalice variants | Enormous scythe reach; rewards spacing and patience |
| Simon's Bowblade | Moderate | The Old Hunters DLC | Adds a ranged option for a Skill/Bloodtinge split |
| Reiterpallasch | Moderate | Cainhurst Castle | A rapier with a built-in firearm, so you can parry mid-combo |

If you are starting fresh, the Threaded Cane is the classic choice — it teaches spacing because you have no choice but to learn it. The Saw Spear is the more forgiving pick, and many players switch to Blades of Mercy the moment Eileen's questline wraps up. Rakuyo is the endgame prize for anyone who wants a pure **Bloodborne skill build** with maximum pressure.

A quick note on variants: Uncanny and Lost versions of these weapons appear in Chalice Dungeons and change which gem slots you get. If your damage feels stuck, a variant swap plus better blood gems usually beats another five levels of Skill.

## Stat Spread and Build Variants at Level 120

Level 120 remains the community's informal dueling bracket, and it is enough to hit 50 Skill without gutting your survivability. The table below shows target ranges rather than exact totals — your final level depends on your starting origin.

| Build Variant | VIT | END | STR | SKL | BLT | ARC | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pure Skill | 45–50 | 25–30 | 10–14 | 50 | Base | 15 | Blades of Mercy, Rakuyo, Threaded Cane |
| Skill / Bloodtinge | 45–50 | 20–25 | 10 | 40 | 35–40 | Base | Chikage, Reiterpallasch, Simon's Bowblade |
| Skill / Arcane | 45 | 25 | 10 | 40 | Base | 25–30 | Blades of Mercy plus Hunter Tools |
| Skill-leaning Quality | 45 | 25 | 25 | 40 | Base | 15 | Burial Blade, Church Pick, hybrid arsenals |

Three rules of thumb:

- **Never level Skill past 50** on a level-capped build. Those points buy almost nothing.
- **Vitality is not optional.** 45–50 VIT is the difference between surviving a boss combo and being deleted during your own attack animation.
- **Strength only to meet requirements.** Unless you are deliberately going quality, put the bare minimum into Strength to equip your weapon.

Endurance is personal. Some hunters swear by 40 for long combo chains; others run 20 and manage stamina through positioning. Start at 25 and adjust based on whether you find yourself backing off to regenerate.

Knowing the numbers is one thing; getting there is another. Here is an order of operations that keeps a **Bloodborne skill build** online from the first hour.

| Stage | Goal | Key Pickups | Notes |

A few practical tips that save hours:

- **Upgrade one weapon to +10 before spreading resources.** A single maxed weapon outperforms three half-upgraded ones.
- **Blood gems matter more than levels.** Three solid physical attack gems can add more damage than ten Skill points past the soft cap.
- **Do not skip the DLC.** Three of the best Skill weapons in the game live there.

## Combat Tips for Parry-Heavy Skill Play

A **Bloodborne skill build** lives and dies on the parry. The Hunter Pistol fires fastest and is the standard choice for reactive parries; the Blunderbuss has a wider spread and is more forgiving at close range. Either way, the rhythm is the same: bait an attack, shoot during the wind-up, then close the gap and press the attack button for a visceral.

Stamina discipline matters more here than on any other build. Skill weapons encourage long combo strings, and running dry mid-string is how hunters die. Two or three hits, then reposition. Transformation attacks are your friend — they often hit harder than a standard swing and let you reset spacing at the same time.

Runes round out the playstyle:

| Rune | Effect | Why It Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Clawmark | Increases visceral attack damage | Stacks with Skill's built-in visceral scaling |
| Oedon Writhe | Restores quicksilver bullets on visceral attacks | Keeps your gun loaded without farming |
| Blood Rapture | Restores HP on visceral attacks | Sustain without spending blood vials |
| Anti-Clockwise Metamorphosis | Raises maximum stamina | Longer combo chains and more dodges |
| Communion | Increases blood vial capacity | Extra safety margin in long boss fights |

Pair Clawmark with a reliable parry and you will see visceral damage numbers that rival a charged Strength attack — from a weapon that swings three times as fast.

## FAQ

**Is a Bloodborne skill build good for a first playthrough?**
Yes, with caveats. The Threaded Cane teaches spacing and the parry loop early, and visceral damage scaling means you are rewarded for learning enemy patterns. The Saw Spear is the more forgiving starting pick if you want an easier first run.

**Which weapon should I use for a Bloodborne skill build at level 120?**
Blades of Mercy and Rakuyo are the community favorites for pure Skill, while the Church Pick and Burial Blade suit players who prefer reach over speed. Pick based on moveset comfort, not attack rating.

**Do I need the DLC for a strong Skill character?**
Not strictly, but it helps enormously. The Old Hunters adds Beasthunter Saif, Church Pick, and Rakuyo — three of the strongest Skill-scaling weapons available.

**How much Strength should a Skill build have?**
Only enough to equip your chosen weapon, typically 10–14. Extra Strength is better spent on Vitality or Endurance unless you are deliberately building a quality hybrid.

**Does Skill increase visceral attack damage?**
Yes — this is the defining advantage of the stat. Every point of Skill raises visceral damage, which is why parry-focused hunters scale so hard into the late game.

Build the character around timing rather than trading, keep your weapon upgraded and gemmed, and the payoff is a hunter who ends fights in two or three clean parries instead of twenty messy swings.
